## How GEMA Powder Coating Guns Work

GEMA powder coating guns use electrostatic technology to charge powder particles and attract them to a grounded metal workpiece. Inside the gun, powder passes through an electrostatic charging zone where a high-voltage corona discharge imparts an electrostatic charge on each particle. Compressed air then propels those charged particles toward the grounded workpiece, where electrostatic attraction holds them in place — including in recessed areas where liquid paint would struggle to penetrate.

This mechanism is what gives powder coating its high transfer efficiency and uniform coverage across complex geometries. The coated part then passes through a curing oven where heat flows the powder and cross-links it into the final hard film.

## GEMA Powder Coating Machines (Control Units)

The control unit — sometimes called the powder coating machine or generator — is the power supply and intelligence hub for the gun system. GEMA control units regulate high-voltage output to the gun, control powder feed rate and air settings, and support recipe programming for different parts and powder types. Advanced units provide fault detection, diagnostics, and connectivity to centralised plant control systems for fully automatic lines.

Selecting the right control unit is as important as selecting the gun itself. The control unit and gun must be matched to the application — wrong pairing leads to inconsistent charging, coating defects, and shortened equipment life.
