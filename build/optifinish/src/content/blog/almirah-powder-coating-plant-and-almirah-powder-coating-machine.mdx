## Why Powder Coating Is the Standard Finish for Almirahs in India

Powder coating has become the default finishing process for steel almirahs, wardrobes, and storage cabinets in India for several well-established reasons.

**Durability** is the primary driver. Powder-coated steel resists scratches, impacts, and humidity far better than liquid paint or stove enamel — qualities that matter in the Indian domestic storage context. The wide colour range available in powder form means manufacturers can offer thousands of RAL and custom colours without managing solvent-based tinting systems. At medium-to-high production volumes, the per-part cost of powder coating is lower than liquid painting, and the process generates no solvent emissions — an advantage for manufacturers in industrial estates with pollution control requirements.

## Types of Almirah Powder Coating Plants

### Manual Almirah Powder Coating Plant

A manual setup is the entry point for small-to-medium almirah manufacturers. Parts are hung on jigs, wheeled or placed into the spray booth, coated with a [GEMA manual gun](/products/gema/manual-gun), and then moved into a batch curing oven.

- **Typical throughput:** 50–200 almirah sets per shift depending on panel sizes and operator efficiency
- **Investment range (2025):** Approximately ₹8–20 lakhs depending on booth size and oven specification
- **Best suited to:** New entrants, small manufacturers, or plants with diverse part types needing manual flexibility

### Semi-Automatic Almirah Powder Coating Line

A semi-automatic setup adds a basic conveyor and may include a vertical reciprocator for the spray zone. Pre-treatment is typically a spray or immersion system. This configuration bridges the gap between manual flexibility and automatic throughput.

- **Typical throughput:** 200–500 almirah sets per shift
- **Investment range (2025):** ₹20–50 lakhs
- **Best suited to:** Growing manufacturers scaling from manual to automatic operation

### Fully Automatic Conveyorised Almirah Line

The fully automatic setup is standard for large almirah manufacturers producing high volumes of standardised panel sizes. Parts move continuously through the line on an overhead conveyor — pre-treatment, drying, powder application, and curing all happen in sequence without manual handling between stages.

- **Typical throughput:** 500–2000+ almirah sets per shift
- **Investment range (2025):** ₹50 lakhs to ₹1.5 crore and above depending on line length and specification
- **Best suited to:** Established manufacturers with high, consistent volume

## The Almirah Powder Coating Process

A properly configured almirah powder coating line moves panels through five stages.

### Stage 1: Pre-Treatment

Almirah steel panels are cleaned of mill scale, rust prevention oil, and welding residues. A phosphating conversion coating is applied to improve corrosion protection and powder adhesion. Pre-treatment quality is the single biggest predictor of coating durability in service.

### Stage 2: Drying

After pre-treatment, panels are dried in a dry-off oven before powder application. Any residual moisture on the surface causes powder adhesion problems that result in blistering or peeling during curing.

### Stage 3: Powder Application

Panels hang on jigs and pass through or are placed in the [spray booth](/products/optifinish-manufactured/powder-spray-booth). GEMA powder coating guns — manual or automatic — apply the charged powder electrostatically to all panel surfaces. Gun selection and booth configuration are matched to the panel geometry.

### Stage 4: Curing

Powder-coated panels cure in the [curing oven](/products/optifinish-manufactured/curing-oven) at 180–200°C. Typical cure time for standard polyester powder on almirah panels is approximately 15 minutes at temperature. Oven temperature uniformity directly affects finish consistency across all panels in a batch.

### Stage 5: Cooling and Packing

Cured panels cool on the line or in a cooling zone before inspection, assembly, and packing. Handling coated panels while still hot risks surface damage.

- **Power supply** — three-phase supply capacity and any local limitations
